THE OPEN PROBLEM

The Open Problem

“The framework tells you what to measure and why. It does not yet tell you what the numbers are — because nobody has measured them.”

This hub closes the way it opened: honestly. Everything built across the last five pages is specified. None of it is validated yet, and that gap is the actual close.

Specified, not validated

The framework’s math is specified, but no fitted parameters exist yet. Every formula in this hub — the shadow price, the token efficiency ratio, the yield curve — describes what to measure and why. None of them yet describes what the numbers actually are for a real, running agentic workload, because that requires production data nobody has collected at scale.

THE CREDIBILITY MOVE

Anyone claiming they already have these numbers is either working in a domain too narrow to generalize, or not being straight about what "measured" means. Admitting the gap is the credible position.

The bound any viable system must satisfy

This is a consequence of the math built across the whole hub, not an opinion about how measurement should work. Any system that wants to actually measure yield for an agentic workload has to clear four bars at once:

  1. decompose tokens by type and by model, rather than treating "tokens" as one commodity;
  2. separate price risk (what the platform charges) from yield risk (what the work is worth);
  3. control for tail concentration, so one expensive run doesn’t distort the average;
  4. make the shadow price μ observable, not just definable on paper.

The discipline behind that honesty

Every claim on this hub is sorted into one of three tiers: documented facts (what the platform actually bills, sourced directly from vendor documentation), arithmetic consequences (what necessarily follows from those facts, regardless of workload), and proposed mechanism and candidate diagnostics (directionally consistent explanations that are not themselves a measurement). Everything in this hub sits in one of those three tiers. The open problem is that almost nothing here has moved past tier one into a validated number yet.

What's genuinely unknown

Three things sit squarely outside the documented-facts tier: a healthy range for the shadow price μ, what counts as a normal Token Efficiency Ratio, and where the quality-sigmoid threshold actually sits for a given domain. All three require production data collected at scale, and none of that collection has happened yet. That includes my own tracking: every measurement item behind this hub is currently not started. Nothing about that is being smoothed over: the only citable numbers anywhere in this hub are the published external evidence cited earlier, not real production numbers.

GO DEEPER — why "observable" is the hard part of the fourth bound

Three of the four bounds above are mostly a matter of discipline: decomposing token types and separating price risk from yield risk are things a team can just decide to do. Making the shadow price μ observable is a different order of problem — it requires per-token-class telemetry tied to task-level outcome data, at production volume, across enough runs that tail-heavy agentic workloads stop distorting the average. That combination doesn’t exist as an off-the-shelf capability today. Building it is the actual work behind "measuring yield," not a footnote to it.

What doesn't wait on the open problem

The measurement gap above is real, but it isn’t the whole picture. Two pages back, three billing surfaces — Copilot, Azure OpenAI, and Claude on Foundry — turned out to expose the same thing this hub keeps needing: per-task-class billing and usage telemetry, not just a total-spend line (see Tokens & the Price You Can’t See). One page later, the Agentic Loop page uses exactly that telemetry to do something with it — a monotonic cost detector that halts and escalates to a human before a budget is exceeded, an accept/reject decision recorded per iteration rather than inferred after the fact from a final diff, and an audit trail that attributes both to a specific task class rather than one blended average.

That chain — platform telemetry, to a per-iteration record, to task-class-level accountability — is what practicing tokenomics with governance means on this hub: not a claim about which vendor is better, but a description of a mechanism that exists because those three surfaces expose the data it runs on. It’s also the one part of this hub that doesn’t wait on the open problem below. An organization doesn’t need a validated shadow price μ to start recording per-task-class accept/reject decisions against real telemetry. That mechanism is available today, on the surfaces already described, independent of whether the yield math two pages back has been fitted to production data yet.

The close

"How do I cut costs" is the wrong question: every dashboard in this hub's first page was already built to answer it. The right one is harder and still open: what would it take to actually measure yield for my team's agentic workload? That's the open problem this hub ends on.
