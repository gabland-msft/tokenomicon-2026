THE OPEN PROBLEM

The Open Problem

“The framework specifies what to measure and why; numerical estimates await measurements that have not yet been made.”

The limits remain those stated on the opening page: everything developed across the previous five pages is specified, not validated, and the measurements needed to test it have not yet been made.

Specified, not validated

The framework’s math is specified, but no fitted parameters exist yet. Each formula in this hub (the shadow price, the token efficiency ratio, the yield curve) describes what to measure and why, and none yet describes what the numbers are for a real, running agentic workload, because that requires production data that nobody has collected at scale.

LIMITS OF EXISTING MEASUREMENTS

Existing claims to these numbers either concern domains too narrow to support generalization or use a different meaning of "measured"; the measurements required here remain unavailable.

The bound any viable system must satisfy

The four requirements below are consequences of the math developed across the hub, independent of any view of how measurement should work, and any system that measures yield for an agentic workload has to meet all four at once:

  1. decompose tokens by type and by model, rather than treating "tokens" as one commodity;
  2. separate price risk (what the platform charges) from yield risk (what the work is worth);
  3. control for tail concentration, so one expensive run doesn’t distort the average;
  4. make the shadow price μ observable in operation as well as definable on paper.

The three tiers of claim status

Every claim on this hub sits in one of three tiers: documented facts (what the platform bills, sourced directly from vendor documentation), arithmetic consequences (what necessarily follows from those facts, regardless of workload), and proposed mechanism and candidate diagnostics (directionally consistent explanations that are not themselves a measurement). Almost nothing here has yet moved past tier one into a measured number, and that is the open problem this page describes.

Quantities not yet measured

Three quantities lie outside the documented-facts tier: a healthy range for the shadow price μ, the value that counts as a normal Token Efficiency Ratio, and the position of the quality-sigmoid threshold for a given domain. All three require production data collected at scale, and none of that collection has happened yet. The only citable numbers anywhere in this hub are the published external evidence cited on earlier pages, and none of them are production numbers from these workloads.

GO DEEPER — why "observable" is the hard part of the fourth bound

Three of the four bounds above are mostly a matter of discipline, since decomposing token types, separating price risk from yield risk and controlling for tail concentration are practices a team can decide to adopt. Making the shadow price μ observable is a harder class of problem, because it requires per-token-class telemetry tied to task-level outcome data, at production volume, across enough runs that tail-heavy agentic workloads stop distorting the average. That combination is not available as an off-the-shelf capability today, and building it is the main body of the work behind "measuring yield."

Governance that can start before yield is measured

The missing measurements above leave one part of this hub unaffected. On the Tokens & the Price You Can’t See page, three billing surfaces (Copilot, Azure OpenAI and Claude on Foundry) were shown to expose what this hub repeatedly requires, namely per-task-class billing and usage telemetry in addition to a total-spend line. The Agentic Loop page that follows it puts that telemetry to use in three ways: a monotonic cost detector that halts and escalates to a human before a budget is exceeded, an accept/reject decision recorded per iteration rather than inferred after the fact from a final diff, and an audit trail that attributes both to a specific task class rather than to one blended average.

On this hub, practicing tokenomics with governance means that chain, running from platform telemetry to a per-iteration record to task-class-level accountability. The mechanism relies on data exposed by the three surfaces and makes no claim about which vendor is better. It also does not depend on the open problem: an organization can record per-task-class accept/reject decisions against real telemetry without a fitted shadow price μ, whether or not the yield math two pages back has been fitted to production data.

The remaining measurement question

The cost dashboards on the opening page already address how to cut spend. The harder question that remains open is what it would take to measure yield for my team's agentic workload.
