MACRO HOOK

The Macro Hook

“You are paying for AI work without a measure of whether it was worth the cost, because existing measures record spend without establishing value.”

This two-minute introduction describes the measurement failure addressed by the hub and sets out the four-stop roadmap developed in the remaining pages.

The $47,000 example

A team spent $47,000 on tokens in a single month, and when asked whether that spend was worth it, nobody on the team could answer. The number was visible to them, but a spend total on its own carries no information about the quality of the work it bought. (The figure is anonymized and illustrative, and is not a real engagement number.)

Cost visibility and value visibility

The team had good cost dashboards, with line items by model, by day and by project, which is what a mature FinOps practice is expected to produce. Those dashboards could not show what the spend got them, measured as output accepted per token, per task or per dollar. Cost visibility and value visibility need two different instruments, and this hub exists because most organizations have built only the first.

THE DISTINCTION BETWEEN SPEND AND VALUE

A spend total is not a value measure: a dashboard can account for every charge without showing whether the money bought useful work. Assessing what one token, one call or one agent run is worth is a unit economics problem rather than a monitoring problem, which only counts what was spent.

The four-theme roadmap

That distinction sets the structure of the rest of the hub, which is organized around a four-theme map, Tokens → Loop → Yield → ?, covering what a token costs, what an agent loop does to that cost, what yield means once cost and outcome are both measured, and a fourth theme left open on purpose (Yield spans two segments below: the reframe and the instruments). The fourth theme remains the hub’s open question. Each of the six segments stands on its own and can be read in any order, and none of them, alone or in sequence, claims to resolve it.

GO DEEPER — differences between cost and yield visibility

FinOps practices have spent years building tooling that answers "what did we spend" precisely, by model, by team and by day, reconciled across invoicing systems; that machinery is mature. It was not designed to answer "what did we get," because that question needs a second axis: an accepted-outcome measure recorded alongside the cost measure, rather than a further cut of the same spend number. A team with a complete cost dashboard can therefore be in the position of the $47,000 team, able to state what left the wallet and unable to say whether it was worth it. The remaining pages of this hub build that second axis.
